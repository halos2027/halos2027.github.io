Scientific Rationale

The (nearly) invisible Halo

The Baryonic Halo contains numerous keys to understanding the evolution of galaxies. In gas, the circumgalactic medium (CGM) holds over 50% of a galaxy's baryons, and serves as a processor for inflowing gas and outflowing material from stellar and AGN feedback. Meanwhile, in stars, structures like shells, streams, and tidal bridges can constrain the accretion and merger history of their hosts.


For many years, these baryonic processes and their tracers have hidden in reaches of the halo — proving challenging to observe due to their low surface brightness, and difficult to simulate due to the many orders of magnitude in scale of the physical processes involved.


Now, new observational facilities, high resolution simulations, and novel computational methods are in the process of revolutionizing the study of the physical processes driving the evolution of galaxies and their halos. We invite the community pushing the boundaries of this field to join us in Chicago, IL USA in June 2027 to share new results, challenges, and approaches to the constraining of this key component of galaxy evolution.
