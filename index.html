Scientific Rationale

The (nearly) invisible Halo

The "island universes" traced by galaxies' visible light belays a complex, dynamic circumgalactic environment. Despite the halo's importance in the baryon cycle and galaxy evolution, much of what exists in the halo is nearly invisible. Now, new instrumentation and technologies, state of the art, high resolution simulations, and novel, extensive surveys are for the first time unlocking significant constraints on the nature of the halo. This conferences focuses on bringing subfield experts together to bridge observations, simulations, and statitical methods at the frontier of this challenging but critical topic of study.

Pillar 01

Dark matter haloes & satellite populations

The dark matter suffusing halos remains frustratingly invisible, but the baryonic tracers of the halo (e.g., satellites, globular clusters, and stellar streams), while faint or challenging to identify, are increasingly being robustly characterized. These observations, along with new simulations, are allowing for new constraints on key properties of dark matter halos and satellite populations:

  • Halo shapes, orientations & the stellar streams that measure them
  • Stream gaps and perturbations as probes of starless subhaloes
  • The subhalo mass function as a dark matter experiment (CDM / SIDM / WDM / fuzzy DM)
  • Satellite infall, ram-pressure stripping & quenching timescales
  • Planes of satellites, group infall & anisotropic accretion
  • Rubin-era dwarf discovery and completeness at the faint limit

Open questions

  • How lumpy is dark matter below the mass where stars form?
  • Can stellar streams probe dark matter substructure?
  • What shapes do real haloes take — and does ΛCDM agree?
  • When, where, and how fast do satellites quench?
  • What drives the scatter in the stellar mass-halo mass relation at low stellar mass?
Pillar 02

Stellar haloes & the buildup of galaxies

Studies of the Milky Way have unveiled dozens of arcing streams of stars. These streams serve as probes of the potential and substructure, and archeological evidence of past accretion. Streams and debris in other galaxies will vastly improve these constraints and allow for holistic views we can't obtain from within the MW: but such measurements require extreme low surface brightness capabilities when targeting other galaxies.

  • Galactic archaeology: chemodynamical tagging of accretion events
  • In-situ vs. accreted haloes — and why the ratio varies so much
  • Deep LSB imaging of external haloes, from Dragonfly to Rubin to Roman
  • Tidal features as a census of minor mergers
  • Disrupted dwarfs, globular cluster streams & the smallest building blocks
  • Connecting Milky Way detail to population statistics

Open questions

  • Can a stellar halo yield its galaxy's full merger tree?
  • What drives the observed diversity in stellar haloes?
  • Where did the stars of the first accreted galaxies end up?
Pillar 03Flagship theme

The circumgalactic medium

The circumgalactic medium (CGM) hosts as much as half of a galaxy's baryons, constantly evolving in a cycle of accretion from the cosmic web, heating and cooling, turbulence and mixing. Gas in the CGM can precipitate down into galaxies to fuel star formation, and galactic feedback drives enriched gas back into the CGM to join the turbulent processes ongoing there. Understanding the CGM and its influence on galaxy evolution is a multiphase challenge, with gas spanning a wide range in temperature and density, at surface brightnesses never before obtained for large samples.

  • The baryon cycle: accretion, winds, fountains & recycling
  • Multiphase structure — cold cloud survival, precipitation & thermal instability
  • The emission-mapping frontier: wide-field IFUs, narrowband & LSB techniques
  • Absorption surveys & tomography of the cool and warm-hot phases
  • Cosmic rays, magnetic fields & non-thermal pressure support
  • Fast radio bursts as a baryon census of halo gas
  • The CGM–quenching connection across mass and environment
  • The resolution frontier: CGM-refinement simulations vs. cloud-scale physics

Open questions

  • Where are the baryons — and in what phase?
  • How does gas actually get into galaxies?
  • What keeps cold clouds alive in hot haloes?
  • Do cosmic rays restructure galactic atmospheres?
  • How does the CGM evolve from high to low redshift?
The throughlines

Bridging methods.

Each pillar confronts its simulations, its observational frontier, and what machine learning and AI can accelerate

Frontier · Simulations

Resolving the halo

From cosmological volumes to cloud-crushing boxes: CGM-refinement schemes, MHD and cosmic-ray physics, satellite stripping at survivable resolution, the CGM is being simulated with unprecedented resolution and new physics.

Frontier · Observations

The deep, wide sky

Surveys from Rubin and Roman, deep emission line maps from KCWI, MUSE, and MOTHRA --- gas in the CGM is increasingly targetable in emission.

Frontier · ML & AI

Inference at scale

Simulation-based inference for streams and satellites, emulators bridging theory and survey volumes, foundation models for spectra and imaging. Machine learning methods and AI hold key accelerations for science with massive, multidimensional data.

Venue & travel

Chicago IL, USA.

Candidate venue · A

SkAI Institute — 875 N Michigan Ave, 38th floor

The NSF–Simons AI Institute for the Sky, high in the former John Hancock Center. Conference sessions with the city and Lake Michigan a thousand feet below — an apt altitude for halo science, and a short walk from the Magnificent Mile, hotels, and the lakefront.

Candidate venue · B

Northwestern University — Evanston campus

CIERA's home campus on the Lake Michigan shore, 30 minutes north of downtown by train. Full university facilities, summer housing options, and a quieter lakeside setting.

Final venue will be confirmed with registration. Both are served by O'Hare (ORD) and Midway (MDW); the CTA runs from both airports to downtown and Evanston.
